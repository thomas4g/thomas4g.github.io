Father's Day Challenge 2026

June 18th, 2026

Introduction

Dear fellow fathers,

This Sunday is "Father's Day". No doubt, after a year half gone to hard work providing for your family, you're ready for your hard-earned rest. You probably busted your butt making sure your wife had a relaxing Mother's day. You watched the kids all day. You made breakfast, maybe even dinner. Mom got a well-deserved break, and now it's your turn.

You're not expecting much, but you're ready to sleep in for once; to have breakfast and some hand-made cards brought to you, and for one precious day, to not be asked for the tenth time to handle that chore your wife's been pestering you about.

I get it.

However, I would like to issue us a challenge this year: a call to renewed vigor in our roles as fathers and husbands.

What if, instead of laying back and receiving our laurels, we instead intensified our efforts to pursue excellence as fathers and husbands? What if we took this Father's Day to study how God calls us to live and lead as fathers and husbands, and reinvigorate our pursuit of God's glory in these roles?

Our Lord Jesus, the ultimate role model of leadership for us as husbands and fathers, came not to be served, but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many. Instead of thinking about how we can be served this Sunday, let's commit to serving - serving our children, our wives, and the body of Christ we fellowship with at church.

What does God's Word say?

What would this look like? First, let's briefly review how God's Word calls us to lead as men, as husbands, and as fathers.

How then shall we live

In light of these truths, how shall we then live? I can't tell you what your weeks or weekends should look like exactly, but here are some suggestions for this Father's Day Weekend 2026.

Father's Day 2026 Challenge

  • Skip your TV watching, video games, or whatever your usual hobbies are tomorrow (Saturday). Take the day to finish all your work, and your family's work. Make it your goal that your family has no work to do on Sunday, the Lord's Day. Pay special attention to the invisible work your wife usually does quietly without any notice.
  • Saturday evening, lead your family in family worship. Review the Scripture passage your Pastor will preach on Sunday. Discuss it with your family. Pray with and for them.
  • Give your wife the evening off - send her to take a bath, or go for a walk, or read in peace and quiet. Put the kids to bed. Clean up after dinner. Make your home a peaceful place for the Lord's Day afternoon.
  • On Sunday, get up early to ensure you can privately commune with God in His word and prayer so that you can be an effective leader of your family. Prepare breakfast and help your family prepare for worship.
  • Get your family to church early so that morning worship isn't stressful and rushed, but peaceful. Help your children read along with God's word. Lead by example by singing loud, and help your children sing along.
  • At home, love your wife sacrificially by letting her take an afternoon nap while you handle the children, lunches, etc.
  • Lead your family back to evening worship to bookend the Sabbath in worship.

Conclusion

Thanks for reading and I hope you will join me in a renewed effort to lead and love our families well. God bless you.
In Christ,

Thomas
